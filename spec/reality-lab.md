# Reality Engineering Lab

## Purpose and search space

The Lab discovers and evaluates candidate strategies and Organizations for connectivity intents. It adapts the Reality Lab/Director/Economic Execution Graph patterns in PaySwap 3.0 for connectivity-specific evidence and authorities.

Explore single provider, multiple providers, community provider groups, task-bounded organizations, resource allocation, plan upgrades, a second SIM/independent link, hardware/access-point investments, wholesale bandwidth, group purchasing, workload scheduling, and keep-current/no-change alternatives. A provider-native eligible path is the baseline; composed alternatives are candidates only when every leg has eligibility, credible terms and permission evidence.

An Organization describes who/what executes a strategy (principals/agents, capability requirements, roles, authority, budgets, resource allocation, policy, communication, commitments, compensation/cost allocation, failure and exit). Strategy describes what should happen. Do not conflate the two.

## Opportunity economics

Each candidate records intent/owner references, evidence snapshot/version, baseline and alternatives, estimated incremental benefits, recurring and one-time costs, support/operating cost, demand/utilization assumptions, service impact, downside scenario, payback/break-even horizon, confidence, expiry, approvals and post-action success metrics.

Compare incremental revenue or justified reliability value against data costs, provider fees, payments, support, power where material, equipment annualization, operating risk and demand uncertainty. Distinguish gross revenue, contribution margin, accrued earnings and settled earnings. Show assumptions. Never guarantee predicted income.

## Lab evaluation ladder

1. Validate evidence freshness, hard constraints, owner permissions and real provider offer provenance.
2. Build typed candidates; always include no-change and current provider-native baseline.
3. Run deterministic calculations and sensitivity analysis against a pinned evidence snapshot.
4. Replay available history; label synthetic/fault injection distinctly.
5. Run shadow evaluation without external effect.
6. If justified, request bounded canary approval with owner/operator consent, budget, duration and stop/rollback conditions.
7. Measure actual outcome, reconcile costs/usage, compare prediction to reality, and record deviations.
8. Promote only through deterministic policy/security/acceptance gates; otherwise roll back or declare inconclusive.

Candidate generation may use models. A decision for a fixed evidence/policy snapshot must be reproducible with declared algorithm/version, features, constraints and tie-break. Inferences record their model/version and provenance but never become canonical network facts. Lab cannot create resource access, order upgrades, bind group members, or authorize payouts.

## Hard constraints, learning and abuse

Hard constraints include permission/legal/operator terms, privacy, service requirement, provider eligibility/trust, resource owner restrictions, data/budget cap, member thresholds, and stop conditions. A candidate violating a hard constraint is ineligible, not just lower-scored.

Store evidence references, Strategy/Organization versions, algorithm/model, cost assumptions, predicted outcomes, approvals, execution correlation, observed outcomes and reconciliation. Do not feed raw customer traffic into prompts where aggregate evidence suffices. Guard against Sybil groups, fake utilization, collusive ratings, inflated demand, reward gaming and manipulated measurements. Keep provider trust, authenticated claims, observation, and model inference distinct.
